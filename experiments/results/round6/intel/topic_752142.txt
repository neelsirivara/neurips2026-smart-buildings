Topic #752142: [Bug report] Boiler gas is not billed while the hot-water setpoint is below the return water temperature (free reheat via a setpoint sawtooth)
  Author: Shamroze Nasir
  Posted: 2026-10-23 19:38:09.573000
  Votes: 6  Comments: 4

Hi organizers,
While testing our policy locally we found what looks like an energy-accounting bug in the boiler model. It lets a policy get reheat without paying for most of the gas. We have not used it in any submission, and we're reporting it so it can be fixed for everyone.
What happens
In smart_control/simulator/boiler.py, compute_thermal_energy_rate() (around line 215):

if self._supply_water_temperature_setpoint &gt; return_water_temp:
    supply_water_temp = self._supply_water_temperature_setpoint
else:
    supply_water_temp = return_water_temp
flow_heating_energy_rate = c_w * rho_w * total_flow_rate * (supply_water_temp - return_water_temp)


When a policy sets the hot-water setpoint below the return water temperature, flow_heating_energy_rate becomes 0.
But the VAV reheat coils (vav.py, output()) use hot_water_system.supply_water_temperature_sensor, which is the tank's current temperature. When the setpoint drops, _adjust_temperature() lowers that temperature only at the fixed cooling_rate (0.1 K/min in the training configs). That rate doesn't depend on how much heat the coils draw.
So the coils keep receiving water at roughly 340–345 K and keep heating the zones, while the boiler bills only:
the tank term (credited as negative while the tank cools), and
the small dissipation term.
A policy that repeatedly raises the setpoint to the maximum and drops it to the minimum (a sawtooth) therefore gets most of its reheat for free.
Reproduction (stock pid_water baseline, only the water setpoint changed)
Run from the competition data folder:

import sys; sys.path.insert(0, ".")
from pathlib import Path
import numpy as np
from smart_buildings import evaluate as ev
from smart_buildings.baselines.pid_plus import PIDWaterResetPolicy

d = Path("smart_buildings/training_scenarios/building_train_8/fairbanks_8")

def run(sawtooth, steps=1152):
    env = ev.load_hybrid_environment(d / "config.gin"); env.metrics_path = None
    env._occupancy_normalization_constant = 125.0
    tr = ev.SimulationTracker(env, vmin=280, vmax=310, capture_snapshots=False)
    md = ev.create_building_meta_data(env, d)
    tank = md["observation_field_names"].index("hws_supply_water_temperature_sensor")
    pol = PIDWaterResetPolicy(md, env.action_spec())
    tr.reset(); obs = tr.last_obs
    for _ in range(steps):
        a = pol.get_action(obs)
        if sawtooth and a["discrete_action"][-1] == 1:
            a["continuous_action"][md["water_indices"]] = -1.0 if obs[tank] &gt;= 345.0 else 1.0
        obs, *_ = tr.step(ev.validate_action(a, env.action_spec()))
    rc = tr.reward_components
    return [np.mean([r[k] for r in rc]) for k in ("normalized_productivity_regret",
            "normalized_energy_cost", "normalized_carbon_emission", "agent_reward")]

print("as shipped:", run(False))
print("sawtooth:  ", run(True))


Results on building_train_8/fairbanks_8, first 1152 steps (4 days):
Policy    Regret  Energy  Carbon  Reward
pid_water as shipped    -0.0513 0.0447  0.0851  -0.0567
pid_water + setpoint sawtooth    -0.0485 0.0330  0.0620  -0.0481
Comfort is the same or slightly better, while energy and carbon drop by about 26–27%. With a well-tuned controller in cold weather, the same trick roughly halves carbon (in our test, carbon went from 0.073 to 0.035 with no change in comfort).
Possible fix
Bill the flow term against the water the coils actually receive, i.e. use the tank's current temperature (supply_water_temperature_sensor) instead of the setpoint when computing flow_heating_energy_rate. A more complete option: let the tank temperature fall according to the heat actually drawn by the coils, (Q_coils − Q_burner)/(ρ·c·V), instead of a fixed cooling_rate.
If this is fixed, could you consider re-scoring existing submissions, so that teams relying on it don't keep an advantage?
Happy to share more details or test a patch. Thanks for organizing the challenge!
— Shamroze Nasir (Mimar AI)
