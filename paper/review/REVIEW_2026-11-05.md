# Review: "Schedule-Aware Demand-Based Reset" (SB26 poster round), 2026-11-05

Reviewed: `paper/main.tex` and the built `main.pdf` (16 pp.: 6 pp. main text, 10 pp. appendix), `results_macros.tex`, `results_macros_r9r10.tex` and the `\input` tables. Line numbers refer to `main.tex`.

## 1. Summary

The paper presents SADR, a hand-built rule-based HVAC controller. It reads the plant topology from the building metadata and otherwise encodes physics shared by every generated SB26 building. The shipped version (v12) scores 0.00745 on the 30 public scenarios, against 0.0550 for the strongest organizer baseline (`pid_water`). The paper also claims that a pre-registered "screen, then confirm on unseen scenarios" protocol kept every shipped change honest, and that Kaggle submissions show the hidden gap comes from weather, not from buildings.

## 3. The ten most important problems (most important first)

### P1. The main contribution is unclear; the paper reads as a development diary. Status: ALREADY RAISED in part (REVIEW_1 pass 5 W6 and pass 6 W5: "Sections 4-5 read as a log of rounds"; never fixed). NEW: the abstract and contributions rewrite, and the version/round numbering mismatch.
- **Where:** Title (l.33); abstract (l.46); contributions (l.55-61); Tables 1-2 (l.136-181); §5 headings "What round 6 found" and "What each heating lever is worth (round 7)" (l.210, 212).
- **What is wrong:** The abstract spends three of its eight sentences on mechanism and two on process. Its last third is about rounds and Kaggle. The reader never gets a single sentence saying what was learned. The main text uses nine version labels and ten round labels, and the version number differs from the round number (v7 = round 4, v9 = round 6, v10 = round 8). Contribution 4 ("every number is generated from the result files") is a reproducibility property, not a contribution.
- **Why a reviewer cares:** Poster judges read the abstract, Figure 1 and Table 1. Right now they see seven SADR rows and a protocol, not an insight.
- **Fix:** Lead with the insight and the result, and push the version history to one table. Suggested abstract (about 170 words):
  > The NeurIPS 2026 Smart Buildings Challenge asks for one HVAC policy that runs procedurally generated office buildings it has never seen, scored on comfort, energy cost and carbon. A rule-based controller built only from the plant topology in the metadata and from physics shared by every generated building scores 0.00745 on the 30 public scenarios (lower is better), about 7x below the strongest organizer baseline (0.0550). Three facts of the benchmark fix most of the design. Comfort costs nothing while the building is empty, so the plant idles until an optimum start. Supply air can only be cooled, so all heat enters through reheat coils, and the gas paid per joule of delivered heat has a closed form. Fan power grows as P^{3/2}, so static pressure is the lowest that fully opens the most demanding damper. From the sixth version on, a change shipped only if it passed a rule fixed in advance on scenarios not used to choose it. The last change lowers the loss by 1.4% on 40 cold scenarios of 8 new buildings (40/40 wins). What remains is mostly gas spent recovering from setback in deep cold. We release the code.
- Rename the §5 headings by content: "Where the gas goes" and "What each heating lever is worth". Drop the round numbers from headings.

### P6. The paper refuses one simulator gap but uses another, and does not say why. Status: ALREADY RAISED in part (pass 4 W4: the weekend floor is below frost-protection practice). NEW: the clash with the billing-gap stance, v12 deepening the floor by a further 6 K, and the billing-gap stance being absent from the main text.
- **Where:** l.210 (v8's coldest weekend zone reaches −2.9 °C; 2.4% of zone-steps below 5 °C; "the simulator does not charge for frost"); l.230; Table 17 `r6_c10_frost5` "frost floor 5 °C (sensible practice)" rejected (+0.9% on HS); Appendix G l.521 (v12 deepens the weekend setback from 6 K to 12 K). The billing-gap stance appears only in the appendix (l.434-438, 525-527).
- **What is wrong:** The author deliberately does not exploit the boiler-billing gap, and that is a real integrity point. But it is invisible in the main text. Meanwhile the shipped policy lowers the weekend floor by a further 6 K below a level that already took zones below freezing, after rejecting a frost floor described as "sensible practice". A judge will see an inconsistent stance unless the principle is stated.
- **Why a reviewer cares:** Judges reward entries that avoid simulator artefacts. Stated clearly, the billing-gap choice is a selling point. Left unstated next to the frost choice, it looks selective.
- **Fix:** Keep the billing-gap framing and move it into the main text, together with the frost disclosure. Replace the "Simulator constants" bullet (l.230) with:
  > \item \textbf{Simulator gaps.} SADR does not use the boiler-billing gap that starter-kit pull request~\#2 closes: in a patched kit v7's loss falls by \RFivePrTwoVSevenRelAbs{} on the six public Fairbanks scenarios while \texttt{pid\_water}'s rises by \RFivePrTwoPidRelAbs, and v12 leaves out a hot-water ramp whose saving came only from that gap. We treat a billing error the organizers have a fix for as out of bounds and the reward as written otherwise. The reward has no frost cost, and SADR's weekend setback uses that: under v8 the coldest zone reached \RSixDecColdestWe\,$^\circ$C, v12 lowers the weekend floor by a further 6\,K, and a 5\,$^\circ$C floor cost \RSixScrCTenHsRelAbs{} on HS (v8). A real deployment needs that floor. SADR also encodes simulator constants (discharge limit, valve $k_v$, constant COP) that a real building would commission.
- Report v12's coldest zone temperature and the cost of a 5 °C floor on v12 (one run of the HS screen).
