Schedule-Aware Demand-Based Reset: A Structured
   Rule-Based Controller for Cross-Building HVAC
       Transfer in the Smart Buildings Challenge

                                                   Neel Sirivara
                                            University of Washington
                                       nsiriv@cs.washington.edu

                                            Abstract

          The NeurIPS 2026 Smart Buildings Challenge asks for one HVAC control policy
           that operates procedurally generated commercial buildings it has never seen, under
           30-day weather strips from five climates, trading occupant comfort against energy
           cost and carbon. We present SADR (Schedule-Aware Demand-based Reset), a
           pure-numpy rule-based controller that reads the plant topology from the building
           metadata and is otherwise built from physics shared by every generated building.
           SADR gates its mode on occupancy and schedule with a self-tuning optimum start,
           regulates each zone with a PI loop on net heat, translates heat into damper and
          valve commands through the supply-air and discharge physics, and resets pressures
           and hot-water temperature from zone demand. On the 30 public scenarios the final
          version (v10) scores 0.00762 on the leaderboard metric (lower is better), against
           0.0550 for the strongest organizer baseline. Eight development rounds each shipped
           only under a rule fixed in advance and confirmed on scenarios no decision touched;
           the last lowers the loss by 0.9% on 44 unseen harsh-weather scenarios (43/44 wins).
           Submissions of fixed versions to Kaggle show that the hidden buildings behave like
           the public ones and that the hidden weather is harsher. We release the controller
           and report where it still loses: deep cold, where gas dominates.

1 Introduction

Learned building controllers have been studied for two decades, yet few run in real buildings;
the recurring obstacles are data-hungry training, fragile robustness and poor generalization across
buildings [Wang and Hong, 2020]. The Smart Buildings Challenge (SB26) isolates the last of
these. A submission is a single policy class, instantiated on held-out buildings drawn from the same
procedural generator as six public ones and scored on 30-day episodes under weather strips it has not
seen [Goldfeder and Rai, 2026]. The observation and action vectors change shape with the building
(K air handlers and V zones expose K+1 on/off commands and 3K+2+2V continuous setpoints),
and the simulator is Google's open-source Smart Buildings simulator [Goldfeder and Sipple, 2023,
Goldfeder et al., 2025].
We take the position that the right first entry in such a benchmark is a structure-aware rule-based
controller: one that reads the plant topology from the metadata and otherwise encodes physics every
generated building shares. It transfers by construction and shows which part of the score is reachable
without learning.

2 Environment and metric

 ท Plant. Air handlers (AHUs) serve up to ten zones each through one VAV box per zone, mix
   return with outside air (recirculation r  0.7) and can only cool: the supply is min(mixed, SAT).
   Zone flow is d Fmax P/Pdesign for damper d  [0.1, 1] and static pressure P  [200, 2000] Pa.
   Fan power is flow ื P/, so it grows as P 3/2 once dampers are open, and the chiller's COP
   is a constant 3.5. Heat comes only from the VAV reheat coils on one gas boiler loop (supply
   310ญ350 K, 0ญ20 bar), whose effectiveness min(1, Cw/Ca, (308.15 - Tsa)/(Tw - Tsa)) caps the
   discharge at 308.15 K. Gas is billed as heat delivered plus a tank loss.

 ท Reward. Each 5-minute step pays 0.6 (comfort shortfall) + 0.2 (normalized energy cost) +
   0.2 (normalized carbon). The comfort shortfall is the occupancy-weighted productivity loss:
   a deviation of  K from the band costs /5 up to 5 K and 1 + (( - 5)/5)2 beyond, normalized
   by total occupancy. It is therefore zero whenever nobody is in the building. Occupants are present
   only on weekday comfort hours (arrivals 06ญ12, departures 13ญ19, none on US holidays), and
   only the building-wide count is observed. The band is 294ญ297 K in comfort hours and 289ญ298 K
   otherwise.

 ท Energy prices. Energy is normalized by a plant maximum that scales with K. In metric units one
   kW of electricity costs about 2.1ื one kW of gas, and one kW of chiller cooling about 0.6ื.

 ท Metric. The leaderboard metric is the mean over scenarios of |mean per-step reward|; lower
   is better. It is dominated by the extreme climates: the organizers' pid_water loses 2.1ื its
   mild-climate mean in Fairbanks.

 ท Baselines. The kit ships bang_bang, fixed_setpoint, pid (per-AHU PID on the mean served-
   zone temperature, plant idle outside work hours) and pid_water (pid plus worst-zone tracking,
   a proportional economizer, proportional reheat and a hot-water reset). pid_water is the strongest
   and our main comparison; there is no learned baseline.

3 Method: SADR

SADR is a single TransferPolicy class. Its only state is per-zone integrals, per-AHU dwell
counters and the optimum-start statistics, and all gains live in one parameter dictionary (Table 10,
Appendix B). Figure 1 shows one control step; Algorithm 1 (Appendix F) gives it in full.

 ท Topology from metadata. Index tables come from the observation field names, the positional
   AHU action lists, the per-VAV action indices and the AHU-to-zone assignments; ranges and
   statistics come from the normalization metadata. Every action slot is written and clipped on every
   step. Nothing depends on the number of zones or handlers, the floor plan or the strip; non-finite
   inputs fall back to schedule values; there are no files, no network and no randomness.

                                                           2
ท Modes. OCCUPIED when the comfort_mode_now flag is set and the building is not detected
  as closed; PRECONDITION when unoccupied but comfort hours start within a lead time (or
  comfort_mode_soon); SETBACK otherwise.
  ญ "Somebody is here" is detected relative to the running minimum of the affinely normalized
     occupancy count. A weekday on which nobody has arrived by 7:00 is a holiday in the kit's
     occupancy model and is treated as unoccupied until the count rises.
  ญ The optimum-start lead is  = 0 + deficit/(r^/s): the worst zone's distance from the com-
     fort band over a recovery-rate estimate r^ (K/h), re-estimated from each morning's recovery
     (exponential average, weight 0.5), with safety factor s = 1.15, clipped to [0.75, 8] h.

ท Zone loops on net heat. Each zone has heating and cooling targets: the sensor band shrunk
  by 0.3 K (occupied) or 0.8 K (preconditioning). Two PI loops compute requested net heat qh =
  kq(Th - T )+ + Ih and cooling qc = kq(T - Tc)+ + Ic in watts (kq = 400 W/K, integral gain
  120 W/K per step). The integrals unwind past the target, so a zone inside the band free-floats, and
  the larger request wins.
  ญ Why net heat, not flow. The zone-air node responds within minutes. A flow-proportional
     controller with a 5-minute step oscillates, whereas a net-heat gain of the order of the air node's
     conductance to the slab is stable, and the integral carries the steady load.
  ญ Setback bursts. A running handler pays the ventilation load of every zone it serves. The floor
     (eco band widened by 10/6 K) is therefore held by requesting 4000 W from any zone that
     crosses it until it is 1 K inside, and the handler is off otherwise. Holding the floor with the PI
     loops instead kept a handler running for 84.9% of the unoccupied steps on the coldest scenario.

ท From heat to actuators. A cooling zone needs capacity rate F = qc/(T - Tsa), Tsa being
  the AHU's estimated supply temperature; a heating zone is bounded by the discharge limit,
  F = qh/(308.15 -  - T ).
  ญ SAT. A cooling handler commands the coldest value, 285 K. The coil load is qc + F (Tmixed - T )
     at a constant COP, so the ventilation term and fan power fall with flow; a Guideline-36 upward
     reset [ASHRAE, 2021, Taylor, 2018] is right in a real building but wrong here. A handler that
     also heats is floored at 292 K, so its cooling zones are not overcooled into reheat; a heating-only
     handler commands 305 K, so the coil idles.
  ญ Static pressure is trim-and-respond in closed form: P = Pdesign maxz fz2 clipped to
     [200, 2000] Pa, with fz each zone's requested fraction of design flow, so the most demanding
     damper is fully open. Dampers are fz/ P/Pdesign.
  ญ Reheat. The discharge temperature that delivers qh at the delivered flow gives the water capacity
     rate Wz = Fz (Tdisch - Tsa)/(Tw - Tsa). The loop differential pressure is reset so the largest
     Wz sits at 0.9 of valve travel, and each valve is Wz/(kv p).
  ญ On/off and air side. An AHU runs iff a served zone requests more than q_on (dwell 3 steps);
     the pump runs iff a valve is open. Recirculation is at the 0.7 cap, except when a cooling-
     only handler sees outside air colder than return air by more than the exhaust-fan break-even
     (0.00322 K/PaืP ); then the outside-air fraction is solved so the mixed air lands on the SAT
     target.
  ญ Hot water. The setpoint is 350 K whenever heating was requested in the last 288 steps and
     310 K otherwise. Gas is billed as heat delivered, so hotter water is strictly more useful at a tank
     dissipation of 0.25 kW.

ท Version changes. v6 scales every zone's gains, request cap, setback burst and optimum start
  with its floor area, read from the metadata zone map, and bounds each request by what the box
  can deliver. v7ญv10 add switches that each reproduce the previous version bit for bit when off
  (Table 2). Appendix C gives the details.

ท Tuning protocol. v4 came from a seeded random search (16 configurations; doubling the integral
  gain to 120 W/K per step is the whole gain, -9.8%), and v5 from 13 physics-motivated candidates
  (Appendix B). Rounds 3ญ7 screened single changes on public and held-out scenarios, combined
  the helpful ones, and shipped only under a rule fixed beforehand: not worse on the public set, and
  better on held-out scenarios at full length. Rounds 4ญ8 wrote the protocol down before running
  anything (PREREG.md) and confirm on scenarios no decision touched: generated buildings (rounds
  3ญ4), the kit's unused Minneapolis station (round 5) and the harshest strips available (rounds 6ญ8).

4 Results

Setup. All runs use the kit's environment loader and SimulationTracker, 8,640 steps per sce-
nario, and the kit's score_leaderboard.py formula, with paired bootstrap CIs over scenarios.

 ท Machines. Rounds 1ญ4 ran on an Apple M5 Pro and a Windows laptop, round 5 on the laptop,
   round 6 on private Kaggle CPU kernels and the M5 Pro, and rounds 7ญ8 on the kernels (round 7's
   zone logs on the M5 Pro).

 ท Reproducibility. The seeds are fixed and runs reproduce across machines: SADR bit for bit
   between laptop and Kaggle (4 of 4 checks), within 10-10 on the M5 Pro in round 6, and pid_water
   within 0.017%.

 ท Baseline returns. Local pid_water returns differ from the shipped baseline_returns.json
   by -1.7% on average, so all comparisons use local runs.

Main result. SADR v10 reaches 0.00762 on the public set, against 0.0550 for pid_water (Table 1;
v7's ratio to pid_water is 0.143, 95% CI 0.113 to 0.176, wins 30/30). Table 2 traces the eight rounds
that produced it.

 ท Every shipped round was confirmed out of sample. Each version from v6 on lowered the loss
   on its confirmation set, which no decision touched (Table 2). On fresh default-range buildings, v8
   ties and v9 does not help (Appendix D).

 ท Gains shrank as the controller matured. The public-set reductions fall from 9.7% (v4  v5) to
   1.6% (v9  v10), and round 7 shipped nothing.

 ท Where the loss goes. Per-step plant energy logs split the loss (Figure 4). On the public set, v5
   pays 52.4% for gas, 38.5% for the chiller, 4.6% for fans and 4.3% for comfort (Fairbanks: gas
   89.9%); pid_water pays 71.5% for comfort.

 ท Held-out buildings. On 40 scenarios of 8 default-range generated buildings (Figure 5), v6 scores
   0.0121, against 0.0137 for v5 and 0.0510 for pid_water.

Kaggle: the hidden gap is weather, not buildings. On the Kaggle public leaderboard (about ten
of 30 hidden scenarios: six buildings from the same generator under five stations absent from the
public set), v7 scores 0.01088, 1.39 times its public-set number, although Kaggle's run of a public
scenario matches the local total to 0.02%. Round 5 submitted fixed files (v6, v5, pid_water) and
compared each local set's ratios to v7 with Kaggle's (Table 16).

 ท Buildings: v5's Kaggle ratio (1.067) equals the public set's (1.058), not the larger default-range
   buildings' (1.169), so the hidden buildings behave like the shipped six.

 ท Weather: pid_water's ratio (5.89) falls in cold weather, and the public set plus Minneapolis
   reproduces it (6.02).

 ท Consequence: rounds 6ญ8 confirmed on harsh-weather strips of the shipped six buildings. A
   mixture of 34% harsh and the rest public, fitted to v8's Kaggle score, predicts pid_water's
   Kaggle ratio (5.81 against 5.96; Table 3).

5 Analysis

Why it wins, and where the loss sits.

 ท pid_water keeps 61.5% of occupied zone-steps outside the band (one loop per handler, a step
   start at 06:00; Figure 6).

 ท SADR v6 keeps 2.3% and uses far less energy: floor pressure with wide dampers, least reheat flow
   at the discharge cap, and no conditioning while nobody is charged for comfort.

 ท For v6, 23.5% of the loss is incurred on unoccupied steps, most at hour 5, the warm-up before
   06:00. Fairbanks is gas (Eq. 1); Phoenix and Singapore are chiller electricity, where the coldest
   supply at the lowest flow is cheapest.

What round 6 found. On HS, v8 pays 95.3% of its loss for gas: 63.9% of it in occupied weekday

hours, 24.3% in weekday preconditioning and 7.0% in weekend setback. The outside-air load of

running handlers equals 0.49 of the gas, 0.43 of it through zones being heated. v8's weekend floor is
about 2C, and the coldest zone reaches -2.9C (2.4% of zone-steps below 5C; the simulator does

not charge for frost). Rounds 3ญ5: Appendix E.

What each heating lever is worth (round 7). From the kit's equations, a box delivers the net
heat Q = cpF (Td - T ) while its coil, which the boiler bills, supplies cpF (Td - Tsa), with
Tsa = min(SAT, r Tret + (1 - r) Tout) and r  0.7. One joule of net heat therefore costs

1.030 times the coil duty).

 ท Two levers are spent. In the check on building 8 in January, the handlers sit at the recirculation
   cap on 100% of their steps, and 82% of the net heat enters at the 308.15 K discharge cap. For
   a zone at 294 K, g is 1.45 at 0C and 2.31 at -40C (1.09 and 1.53 for a zone at 281 K). What
   control still chooses is when, and at which zone temperature, heat goes in.

 ท A zone model. Fitted on 306 zones of six harsh scenarios, a first-order model T = aQ - k(T -
   Tout) + c has a median time constant of 33 h (9ญ192 h). It explains only 0.41 of the 30-minute
   variance (median) and predicts a recovery's duration with a median error of 27%. At the cap, the
   recovery rate falls by 0.60 K/h for each K the zone is warmer.

 ท Setback depth. In this model a deeper setback floor never costs gas if the zone reaches it and
   recovers in time (its trajectory lies below the shallower one, and both the losses and g grow with
   T ). Depth therefore pays only where the floor binds, on 1%ญ47% of unoccupied zone periods
   under v9.

 ท Start time. With capped heat the latest feasible start is optimal, so a start rule can save at most the
   time zones hold at the target before 06:00: 0.36 h per recovery, 2.6% of the gas.

 ท Checks. Seven-day checks on three harsh scenarios confirm this where the model fits. A 2 K
   deeper floor changes the gas by -1.1% (predicted -1.0%) in building 8, where the floor binds most,
   and -0.9% (-0.9%) in building 7, but +0.1% (-0.4%) in building 1, whose zones cool slowest. A
   lead safety of 1.0 changes the gas by -2.2% in building 7 (predicted -2.0%), but the loss only by
   -1.2%: part returns as comfort.

6 Limitations and disclosure

 ท Deep cold. SADR loses most in deep cold: v7's worst public scenario scores 0.0461, almost all
   gas, about half of it outside-air overhead.

 ท Simulator constants. SADR encodes simulator constants (discharge limit, valve kv, constant
   COP) that a real building would commission. Its weekend floor ignores frost protection; a 5C
   floor costs 0.9% on HS.

 ท Selection. Selection used the public set and screens, so the public number is optimistic; the
   out-of-sample confirmations in Table 2 are the honest estimates.

 ท Next step. MPC or a learned residual on top of SADR.
