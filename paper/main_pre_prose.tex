\begin{abstract}
The NeurIPS 2026 Smart Buildings Challenge asks for one HVAC control policy that runs procedurally generated office buildings it has never seen, under 30-day weather strips from five climates, scored on occupant comfort, energy cost and carbon. SADR (Schedule-Aware Demand-Based Reset) is a deterministic rule-based controller built only from the plant topology in the building metadata and from physics shared by every generated building. On the 30 public scenarios it scores \RTenConfPubCand{} (lower is better), \RElvPwTimes{} times below the strongest organizer baseline (\MetricPidWater; lower on \RElvPwWins{} scenarios). Three facts of the benchmark fix most of the design. Comfort costs nothing while the building is empty, so the plant idles until an optimum start. Supply air can only be cooled, so all heat enters through reheat coils, and the gas paid per joule of delivered heat has a closed form. Fan power grows as $P^{3/2}$, so static pressure is the lowest that fully opens the most demanding damper. A leave-one-out ablation measures what each mechanism of the shipped version is worth. From v6 on, a change shipped only if it passed a rule fixed in advance on scenarios not used to choose it (one round reused an earlier confirmation set); the last change lowers the loss by \RTenConfHarshCThreeRelAbs{} on \RTenConfHarshCThreeN{} cold scenarios of \RTenNNewBuild{} new buildings (\RTenConfHarshCThreeWins{} wins). Kaggle scores of earlier versions suggest that the hidden buildings resemble the public ones and that the hidden weather is colder; the shipped version has not been scored there. What remains is mostly gas spent in deep cold. SADR does not use a known gas-billing gap in the simulator, and the code is released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Learned building controllers have been studied for two decades, yet few run in real buildings; the recurring obstacles are data-hungry training, fragile robustness and poor generalization across buildings~\citep{wang2020rl}. The Smart Buildings Challenge (SB26) isolates the last of these. A submission is a single policy class, instantiated on held-out buildings drawn from the same procedural generator as six public ones and scored on 30-day episodes under weather strips it has not seen~\citep{sb26kit}. The observation and action vectors change shape with the building ($K$ air handlers and $V$ zones expose $K{+}1$ on/off commands and $3K{+}2{+}2V$ continuous setpoints), and the simulator is Google's open-source Smart Buildings simulator~\citep{goldfeder2023lightweight,goldfeder2025suite}.

We take the position that the right first entry in such a benchmark is a \emph{structure-aware rule-based controller}: one that reads the plant topology from the metadata and otherwise encodes physics every generated building shares. It is designed to transfer, since nothing in it is fitted to a particular building, and we test transfer on generated buildings it never saw. It also shows which part of the score is reachable without learning.

\paragraph{Related work.} Building-control benchmarks such as BOPTEST~\citep{blum2021boptest}, CityLearn~\citep{vazquezcanteli2019citylearn} and Sinergym~\citep{jimenezraboso2021sinergym} pair learned and model-based controllers with tuned rule-based references, and model-predictive control (MPC) with weather forecasts is the best-studied model-based alternative~\citep{oldewurtel2012mpc,drgona2020mpc}. Practice follows ASHRAE Guideline~36's trim-and-respond resets~\citep{ashrae2021g36,taylor2018vav}. SADR sits between practice and MPC: it keeps Guideline~36's demand-driven structure but derives each reset from the simulator's own equations. Two features of SB26 change the usual trade-offs. The buildings are unseen, so nothing can be identified offline, and comfort is charged only for occupants present, so an empty building may drift as far as it can recover in time.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Three design facts} of the SB26 reward and plant that fix most of the controller (Section~\ref{sec:env}).
\item \textbf{SADR}, a deterministic numpy controller built from them (\PolicyMsOurs{}\,ms per step), \RElvPwTimes{} times below \texttt{pid\_water} on the public scenarios, with a leave-one-out ablation of every mechanism (Sections~\ref{sec:method} and~\ref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{A shipping protocol}: screens, loss decompositions and pre-registered full-length confirmations on scenarios not used to choose the change, with Kaggle submissions used to find which local set predicts the hidden scenarios (Table~\ref{tab:rounds}).
\item \textbf{Where the loss sits, and pitfalls for other entrants} (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}): gas in deep cold, a day-of-week decoding error that makes Saturday a workday, and organizer baseline returns that differ from local runs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Environment and metric}
\label{sec:env}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Plant.} Air handlers (AHUs) serve up to ten zones each through one VAV box per zone, mix return with outside air (recirculation $r\le 0.7$) and can only cool: the supply is $\min(\text{mixed}, \text{SAT})$. Zone flow is $d\,F_{\max}\sqrt{P/P_{\text{design}}}$ for damper $d\in[0.1,1]$ and static pressure $P\in[200,2000]$\,Pa. Fan power is $\text{flow}\times P/\eta$, so it grows as $P^{3/2}$ once dampers are open, and the chiller's COP is a constant 3.5. Heat comes only from the VAV reheat coils on one gas boiler loop (supply 310--350\,K, 0--20\,bar), whose effectiveness $\min(1, C_w/C_a, (308.15-T_{\text{sa}})/(T_w - T_{\text{sa}}))$ ($C_w$, $C_a$: water- and air-side capacity rates; $T_w$: supply water temperature) caps the discharge at 308.15\,K. Gas is billed as heat delivered plus a tank loss.
\item \textbf{Reward.} Each 5-minute step pays $0.6\,\text{(comfort shortfall)} + 0.2\,\text{(normalized energy cost)} + 0.2\,\text{(normalized carbon)}$. The comfort shortfall is the occupancy-weighted productivity loss: a deviation of $\Delta$\,K from the band costs $\Delta/5$ up to 5\,K and $1+((\Delta-5)/5)^2$ beyond, normalized by total occupancy. It is therefore \emph{zero whenever nobody is in the building}. Occupants are present only on weekday comfort hours (arrivals 06--12, departures 13--19, none on US holidays), and only the building-wide count is observed. The band is 294--297\,K in comfort hours and 289--298\,K otherwise.
\item \textbf{Energy prices.} Energy is normalized by a plant maximum that scales with $K$. In metric units one kW of electricity costs about $2.1\times$ one kW of gas, so one kW of chiller cooling (COP 3.5) costs about $0.6\times$ one kW of gas.
\item \textbf{Metric.} The leaderboard metric is the mean over scenarios of $|\text{mean per-step reward}|$; lower is better. It is dominated by the extreme climates: the organizers' \texttt{pid\_water} loses \ExtremeRatioFairbanks$\times$ its mild-climate mean in Fairbanks.
\item \textbf{Baselines.} The kit ships \texttt{bang\_bang}, \texttt{fixed\_setpoint}, \texttt{pid} (per-AHU PID on the mean served-zone temperature, plant idle outside work hours) and \texttt{pid\_water} (\texttt{pid} plus worst-zone tracking, a proportional economizer, proportional reheat and a hot-water reset). \texttt{pid\_water} is the strongest and our main comparison; there is no learned baseline.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Three design facts.} (1) \emph{Comfort is free while the building is empty}, so the plant should idle and start as late as recovery allows. (2) \emph{Supply air can only be cooled}, so all heat enters through the reheat coils at the 308.15\,K discharge cap, and the gas paid per joule of net heat has a closed form (Eq.~\ref{eq:g}). (3) \emph{Fan power grows as $P^{3/2}$} and the chiller's COP is constant, so the static pressure should be the lowest that fully opens the most demanding damper, and a cooling handler should supply its coldest air at the lowest flow.

\section{Method: SADR}
\label{sec:method}

SADR is a single \texttt{TransferPolicy} class. Its only state is per-zone integrals, per-AHU dwell counters and the optimum-start statistics, and all gains live in one parameter dictionary (Table~\ref{tab:params}, Appendix~\ref{app:tuning}). Figure~\ref{fig:sadr} shows one control step; Algorithm~\ref{alg:sadr} (Appendix~\ref{app:figs}) gives it in full.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Topology from metadata.} Index tables come from the observation field names, the positional AHU action lists, the per-VAV action indices and the AHU-to-zone assignments; ranges and statistics come from the normalization metadata. Every action slot is written and clipped on every step. Nothing depends on the number of zones or handlers, the floor plan or the strip; non-finite inputs fall back to schedule values; there are no files, no network and no randomness.
\item \textbf{Modes.} \textsc{Occupied} when the \texttt{comfort\_mode\_now} flag is set and the building is not detected as closed; \textsc{Precondition} when unoccupied but comfort hours start within a lead time (or \texttt{comfort\_mode\_soon}); \textsc{Setback} otherwise.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item ``Somebody is here'' is detected relative to the running minimum of the affinely normalized occupancy count. A weekday on which nobody has arrived by \ParHolidayCheckH{}:00 is a holiday in the kit's occupancy model and is treated as unoccupied until the count rises.
  \item The optimum-start lead is $\ell = \ell_0 + \text{deficit}/(\hat{r}/s)$ ($\ell_0 = \RElvParLeadBaseH$\,h): the worst zone's distance from the comfort band over a recovery-rate estimate $\hat{r}$ (K/h), re-estimated from each morning's recovery (exponential average, weight \ParRateEma), with safety factor $s=\ParLeadSafety$, clipped to $[\ParLeadMinH, \ParLeadMaxH]$\,h.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Zone loops on net heat.} Each zone has heating and cooling targets: the sensor band shrunk by \ParMarginOcc\,K (occupied) or \ParMarginPre\,K (preconditioning). Two PI loops compute requested net heat $q_h = k_q (T_h^\star - T)^+ + I_h$ and cooling $q_c = k_q (T - T_c^\star)^+ + I_c$ in watts ($k_q = \ParKqWPerK$\,W/K, integral gain \ParKiWPerKStep\,W/K per step). The integrals unwind past the target, so a zone inside the band free-floats, and the larger request wins.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Why net heat, not flow.} The zone-air node responds within minutes. A flow-proportional controller with a 5-minute step oscillates, whereas a net-heat gain of the order of the air node's conductance to the slab is stable, and the integral carries the steady load.
  \item \emph{Setback bursts.} A running handler pays the ventilation load of every zone it serves. The floor (the out-of-hours band of 289--298\,K widened by \ParNightLowExt{}/\ParNightHighExt\,K) is therefore held by requesting \ParQSbW\,W from any zone that crosses it until it is \ParSbHystK\,K inside, and the handler is off otherwise. Holding the floor with the PI loops instead kept a handler running for \VthreeAhuOnUnocc{} of the unoccupied steps on the coldest scenario.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{From heat to actuators.} A cooling zone needs capacity rate $F = q_c/(T - T_{\text{sa}})$, $T_{\text{sa}}$ being the AHU's estimated supply temperature; a heating zone is bounded by the discharge limit, $F = q_h/(308.15 - \delta - T)$, with a discharge margin $\delta$ (\ParDischMarginK\,K in v12).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{SAT.} A cooling handler commands the coldest value, \ParSatCoolHi\,K. The coil load is $q_c + F\,(T_{\text{mixed}} - T)$ at a constant COP, so the ventilation term and fan power fall with flow; a Guideline~36 upward reset~\citep{ashrae2021g36,taylor2018vav} is right in a real building but wrong here. A handler that also heats is floored at \ParSatMixFloor\,K, so its cooling zones are not overcooled into reheat; a heating-only handler commands 305\,K, so the coil idles.
  \item \emph{Static pressure} is trim-and-respond in closed form: $P = P_{\text{design}}\max_z f_z^2$ clipped to $[200, 2000]$\,Pa, with $f_z$ each zone's requested fraction of design flow, so the most demanding damper is fully open. Dampers are $f_z/\sqrt{P/P_{\text{design}}}$.
  \item \emph{Reheat.} The discharge temperature that delivers $q_h$ at the delivered flow gives the water capacity rate $W_z = F_z\,(T_{\text{disch}} - T_{\text{sa}})/(T_w - T_{\text{sa}})$. The loop differential pressure is reset so the largest $W_z$ sits at \ParValveTarget{} of valve travel, and each valve is $W_z/(k_v \sqrt{\Delta p})$, $k_v$ being the valve's flow coefficient at full opening.
  \item \emph{On/off and air side.} An AHU runs iff a served zone requests more than $q_{\text{on}} = \RElvParQOnW$\,W (dwell \ParMinOnSteps{} steps); the pump runs iff a valve is open. Recirculation is at the 0.7 cap, except when a cooling-only handler sees outside air colder than return air by more than the exhaust-fan break-even ($0.00322\,\text{K/Pa}\times P$); then the outside-air fraction is solved so the mixed air lands on the SAT target.
  \item \emph{Hot water.} The setpoint is 350\,K whenever heating was requested in the last \ParHeatSeasonSteps{} steps (v12: \RTenSeasonSteps{} while the outside air fell below \RTenSeasonOatC\,$^\circ$C within a day) and 310\,K otherwise. Gas is billed as heat delivered, so hotter water costs only a tank dissipation of \TankLossExtraKw\,kW while widening the coils' reach; in the ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation12}) 330\,K instead changes the public loss by \RElvAbTwRel, so the setpoint hardly matters there.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scaling with the building (v6).} Every zone's gains, request cap, setback burst and optimum start scale with its floor area, read from the metadata zone map (clipped to $[\ParAreaScaleMin, \ParAreaScaleMax]\times$ a \ParAreaRef\,m$^2$ zone), and each request is bounded by what the box and its coil can deliver.
\item \textbf{Additions after v6.} Each is a switch; off reproduces the previous version bit for bit (Appendices~\ref{app:versions} and~\ref{app:note-v11}). (i) Occupied flow above the 200\,Pa floor only in proportion to the zone's error (v7). (ii) A night flush with outside air at least \ParRFFlushDt\,K colder, outside heating season (v7). (iii) In heating season the forced minimum air of set-back zones on a running handler is reheated, which buys heat at 1\,J of gas per joule: to zone temperature (v7), and to the discharge limit before a workday and near the floor (v9). (iv) A weekend setback \RTenWkExtLo\,K deeper than the weeknight one far from the next workday (v8: \RTenWkExtLoVEleven\,K; v12: \RTenWkExtLo\,K). (v) A request cap of \RFiveParQcapKw\,kW per 100\,m$^2$ (v8). (vi) An online zone model, first order in v10 and second order in v12, that may only \emph{shorten} the learned start. (vii) The day of week rounded after decoding (v11), because float32 sine and cosine decode Saturday as 4.999999999, which made it a workday. (viii) A heating season that keeps the hot-water loop hot for \RTenSeasonDays{} days after the last heating demand while the outside air fell below \RTenSeasonOatC\,$^\circ$C within the last day (v12).
\item \textbf{Tuning protocol.} v4 came from a seeded random search (\NTuneConfigs{} configurations; doubling the integral gain to \ParKiWPerKStep\,W/K per step alone changes the screening loss by $\AblRelDefAddKi$, against $-$\RElvTuneGain{} for the whole search), and v5 from \NCandV{} physics-motivated candidates (Appendix~\ref{app:tuning}); both were selected on the public set. From round 3 on, single changes were screened on public and held-out scenarios, the helpful ones combined, and a version shipped only under a rule fixed beforehand: not worse on the public set, and better at full length on scenarios not used to choose it. Rounds 4--10 were pre-registered (\texttt{PREREG.md}). The confirmation sets were generated buildings (rounds 3--4), the kit's unused Minneapolis station (round 5), the harshest strips available (rounds 6 and 8; round 9 reused round 8's set, Appendix~\ref{app:note-v11}) and new buildings (round 10).
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Setup.} All runs use the kit's environment loader and \texttt{SimulationTracker}, 8,640 steps per scenario, and the kit's \texttt{score\_leaderboard.py} formula, with paired bootstrap CIs over scenarios.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Machines.} Rounds 1--4 ran on an Apple M5 Pro and a Windows laptop, round 5 on the laptop, rounds 6--9 on private Kaggle CPU kernels (6, 7, 9 partly on the M5 Pro), round 10 on the M5 Pro alone: \RTenMacRuns{} runs of \RTenSteps{} steps (\RTenMacConfRuns{} to confirm), \texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS=\RTenOmpThreads}, at most \RTenMacWorkersMax{} at once.

\end{itemize}

\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Why it wins, and where the loss sits.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Figure~\ref{fig:hours12}: \texttt{pid\_water}'s loss is mostly comfort regret in occupied hours. v12's is energy, and its largest hourly loss falls at hour \RElvHourPeak{}, the end of the morning warm-up; \RElvUnoccHourShare{} of it falls outside 06:00--19:00.
\item \texttt{pid\_water} keeps \OccOutPidWater{} of occupied zone-steps outside the band (one loop per handler, a step start at 06:00; Figure~\ref{fig:hours}).
\item SADR v6 keeps \OccOutOurs{} and uses far less energy: floor pressure with wide dampers, least reheat flow at the discharge cap, and no conditioning while nobody is charged for comfort.
\item For v6, \UnoccShareOurs{} of the loss is incurred on unoccupied steps, most at hour \PeakLossHourOurs{}, the warm-up before 06:00. Fairbanks is gas (Eq.~\ref{eq:g}); Phoenix and Singapore are chiller electricity, where the coldest supply at the lowest flow is cheapest.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Where the gas goes in harsh weather (v8).} On HS, v8 pays \RSixDecGas{} of its loss for gas: \RSixDecOccWd{} of it in occupied weekday hours, \RSixDecPreWd{} in weekday preconditioning, \RSixDecPreWe{} in weekend preconditioning and \RSixDecSbWe{} in weekend setback. The outside-air load of running handlers equals a fraction \RSixDecVent{} of the gas, \RSixDecVentHeated{} of it through zones being heated. Frost: Section~\ref{sec:limits}; rounds 3--5: Appendix~\ref{app:diag}.

\paragraph{What each heating lever is worth.}
From the kit's equations, a box delivers the net heat $Q=\rho c_p F (T_d - T)$ while its coil, which the boiler bills, supplies $\rho c_p F (T_d - T_{sa})$, with $T_{sa}=\min(\text{SAT}, r\,T_{\text{ret}} + (1-r)\,T_{\text{out}})$ and $r\le 0.7$. One joule of net heat therefore costs
\begin{equation}
g = \frac{T_d - T_{sa}}{T_d - T} = 1 + \frac{T - T_{sa}}{T_d - T}
\label{eq:g}
\end{equation}
joules of coil duty, which holds in the simulator to within \RSevGErr{} (the kit's methods wrapped); billed gas is \RSevGasOverCoil{} times the coil duty.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two levers are spent.} In the check on building~8 in January, the handlers sit at the recirculation cap on \RSevRecircCapShare{} of their steps, and \RSevCapHeatShare{} of the net heat enters at the 308.15\,K discharge cap. For a zone at 21$^\circ$C, $g$ is \RSevGZero{} at 0$^\circ$C outside and \RSevGMinusForty{} at $-40^\circ$C (\RSevGColdZero{} and \RSevGColdMinusForty{} for a zone at 8$^\circ$C). What control still chooses is when, and at which zone temperature, heat goes in.
\item \textbf{A zone model.} Fitted on \RSevNZones{} zones of six harsh scenarios, a first-order model $\dot T = aQ - k(T - T_{\text{out}}) + c$ has a median time constant of \RSevTauMed\,h (\RSevTauLo--\RSevTauHi\,h). It explains only \RSevRTwo{} of the 30-minute variance (median) and predicts a recovery's duration with a median error of \RSevRecErr. At the cap, the recovery rate falls by \RSevRateSlope\,K/h for each K the zone is warmer.
\item \textbf{Setback depth.} In this model a deeper setback floor never costs gas if the zone reaches it and recovers in time (its trajectory lies below the shallower one, and both the losses and $g$ grow with $T$). Depth pays only where the floor binds, on \RSevBindLo--\RSevBindHi{} of unoccupied zone periods under v9.
\item \textbf{Start time.} With capped heat the latest feasible start is optimal, so a start rule can save at most the time zones hold at the target before 06:00: \RSevHoldH\,h per recovery, \RSevHoldGas{} of the gas.
\item \textbf{Checks.} Twelve seven-day checks on three harsh scenarios test the model (all in Table~\ref{tab:checks_r7}). Floor predictions hold within 0.5 points in buildings~7 and~8 and fail in building~1, whose zones cool slowest: a 2\,K deeper floor changes the gas by $\RSevChkEightNleMeas$ (predicted $\RSevChkEightNlePred$) in building~8, where the floor binds most, but by $\RSevChkOneNleMeas$ (predicted $\RSevChkOneNlePred$) in building~1. Start-time predictions overstate the saving in buildings~8 and~1; in building~7 a lead safety of 1.0 changes the gas by $\RSevChkSevenLsOMeas$ (predicted $\RSevChkSevenLsOPred$) but the loss only by $\RSevChkSevenLsOLoss$, because part returns as comfort.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Takeaways for SB26 entrants.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Round the decoded day of week: float32 sine and cosine put Saturday at 4.999999999, so a test ``day $<5$'' makes it a workday (v11 fixed this, Table~\ref{tab:rounds}).
\item Comfort is free when the building is empty: idle the plant and start it as late as recovery allows.
\item Under the constant COP the coldest supply air at the lowest flow is cheapest; Guideline~36's upward reset is wrong here.
\item Set static pressure so the most demanding damper is fully open.
\item Re-run the baselines locally: the shipped returns differ from local runs by up to $\GapMinPidWater$ per scenario.
\item Harsh-weather sets predict the Kaggle score better than the public set does.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and disclosure}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Deep cold.} SADR loses most in deep cold: v12's worst public scenario, \RElvWorstPubScen, scores \RElvWorstPub, \RElvWorstPubComfortShare{} of it comfort and the rest almost all gas.
\item \textbf{Simulator gaps.} SADR does not use the boiler-billing gap that starter-kit pull request~\#2 closes. In a patched kit v7's loss falls by \RFivePrTwoVSevenRelAbs{} on the \RFivePrTwoVSevenN{} public Fairbanks scenarios while \texttt{pid\_water}'s rises by \RFivePrTwoPidRelAbs, and v12 leaves out a hot-water ramp whose saving came only from that gap (Appendix~\ref{app:note-v11}). We treat a billing error the organizers have a fix for as out of bounds and take the reward as written otherwise. The reward has no frost cost, and SADR's weekend setback uses that. Under v8 the coldest zone on HS reached $\RSixDecColdestWe^\circ$C (\RSixDecBelowFive{} of zone-steps below 5$^\circ$C); v12 sets the weekend floor a further 6\,K lower and reaches $\RElvColdestHS^\circ$C (\RElvBelowFiveHS{} of zone-steps below 5$^\circ$C). A 5$^\circ$C frost floor costs v12 \RElvFrostHsRel{} on HS (95\% CI \RElvFrostHsLo{} to \RElvFrostHsHi). A real deployment needs that floor, and it would also commission the simulator constants SADR encodes (discharge limit, valve $k_v$, constant COP).
\item \textbf{Unconfirmed parts.} v12 has no Kaggle score (the latest scored version, v10, scores \REigKagVTen). Its \RTenSeasonOatC\,$^\circ$C heating-season gate is supported only by development runs, because the cold confirmation windows never exercise it.
\item \textbf{Trade-off.} v12 lowers energy and carbon by accepting \RTenComfRiseHCThree{} more comfort regret on HC3 (\RTenComfRisePub{} on the public set).
\item \textbf{Larger buildings.} On the default-range buildings of FD the gains shrink or vanish: v9 does not help, and \RNineFDTopTwoLosses{} of v11's \RNineConfFreshDefLosses{} losses there fall on its two largest-room buildings.
\item \textbf{Selection.} Selection used the public set and screens, so the public number is optimistic. Table~\ref{tab:rounds}'s confirmations estimate the size of each change, and Kaggle estimates the level.
\item \textbf{Next step.} A model-predictive controller on the kit's own linear building model, or a learned residual on top of SADR. A perfect-foresight linear programme on that model saves \RElvLpMin--\RElvLpMax{} (mean \RElvLpMean) of v10's loss on \RElvLpN{} heating scenarios (Appendix~\ref{app:note-v11}), mostly on Saturday mornings (the bug v11 fixed), in holding the weekend floor and in timing weeknight recovery; how much of the rest is reachable without foresight is open.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility and disclosure.} \texttt{experiments/src/} and \texttt{paper/make\_macros\_*.py} generate the numbers in the text and the generated tables (seeds fixed, CPU only), and \texttt{paper/AUDIT.md} checks each against its source file. A few appendix tables were typed from the result files and are checked there too. An LLM coding assistant (Claude) wrote code and drafted this text. Code and policy: \url{https://github.com/neelsirivara/neurips2026-smart-buildings}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Version changes v6 and v7 in detail}
\label{app:versions}

\paragraph{Scaling with the building (v6).}
Every VAV box in the generator has the same design flow (2\,m$^3$/s) and hot-water $k_v$, so
a 450\,m$^2$ zone has a fifth of the heating authority per square metre of a 100\,m$^2$ zone
and several times its thermal mass; v5's fixed 12\,kW cap, gains and building-wide lead were
tuned on the public zones (median \PubZoneAreaMedian\,m$^2$). v6 (i) reads each zone's floor
area from the metadata zone map (4-connected free cells in raster order reproduce the
simulator's room numbering) and scales $k_q$, $k_i$, the request cap and the setback burst with
area/\ParAreaRef\,m$^2$, clipped to $[\ParAreaScaleMin, \ParAreaScaleMax]$; (ii) bounds each
request by what the box delivers at a flow fraction $f_{\text{cap}}$ (\VSixFCapPre{} while
preconditioning or set back, \ParFCapOcc{} occupied) at the discharge limit, at 285\,K supply,
and on the water side (valve fully open at 20\,bar: asking for more raises the airflow, cools
the discharge and delivers \emph{less}); (iii) runs the optimum start per zone; (iv) raises the
setback floor of a zone whose learned rate could not recover it within the maximum lead. With
these off the code reproduces v5 bit for bit.

\paragraph{Round 4 (v7).}
\label{sec:v7}
Five switches of the v6 code (all off reproduces v6 bit for bit): (i) discharge margin $\delta$ from
\VSixDischMargin{} to \ParRFDischMargin\,K (at the cap the gas per joule of net heat is
$1 + 0.3\,(T - T_{\text{out}})/(308.15 - \delta - T)$); (ii) occupied flow above the 200\,Pa floor only in
proportion to the zone's error (from \ParRFOccEzero\,K, full \ParRFOccSpan\,K later); (iii) a night flush with
outside air at least \ParRFFlushDt\,K colder, outside heating season; (iv) in heating season the forced minimum
flow of set-back zones on a running handler is reheated to the zone temperature (1\,J of gas per joule instead
of the ventilated recovery's 1.9 later); (v) the preconditioning cap at the floor-pressure flow.

\section{Diagnoses of rounds 3--5}
\label{app:diag}
\paragraph{What rounds 3 and 4 found.}
v6 cut the largest-quartile recovery of v5's 350--430\,m$^2$ zones from \DecHeldRVFiveRecoveryBig\,h to
\DecHeldROursRecoveryBig\,h. The generated buildings' larger chiller term is a normalization effect: they cool
\RFCoolWmRThree{} against \RFCoolWmPub\,W/m$^2$ but have \RFAreaPerAhuRThree{} instead of \RFAreaPerAhuPub\,m$^2$ of zone per handler.

\paragraph{What round 5 found.}
Under the Minneapolis strips (set M) v7 pays \RFiveDecMinnGasShare{} of its loss for gas and
\RFiveDecMinnComfortShare{} for comfort, \RFiveCapComfortShareOfMinn{} of it in one January scenario whose
\RFiveCapNZones{} worst zones sat \RFiveCapDefMin--\RFiveCapDefMax\,K below the band because the request cap
(\RFiveCapReqKwMin--\RFiveCapReqKwMax\,kW) bound at about half of what box and coil deliver (v8 lifts it). Handlers
start \RFiveAhuStartsMin--\RFiveAhuStartsMax{} times a day, so cycling is not a lever. Starter-kit pull request~\#2
(unmerged) bills boiler gas from a tank energy balance and closes a gap that rewards saw-toothing the hot-water
setpoint (heat billed at 0.38 of its value); in a patched kit v7's loss falls by \RFivePrTwoVSevenRelAbs{} on the
\RFivePrTwoVSevenN{} public Fairbanks scenarios while \texttt{pid\_water}'s rises by \RFivePrTwoPidRelAbs: SADR's score
does not rest on that gap.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{SADR, one step (vectorized over zones $z$ and handlers $k$)}
\label{alg:sadr}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State mode $\gets$ \textsc{Occupied} / \textsc{Precondition} / \textsc{Setback} from flags, time, occupancy count, lead $\ell$
\State $(T_h^\star, T_c^\star)_z \gets$ band for the mode; update $I_h, I_c$; $q_h, q_c \gets$ PI requests (W), or setback bursts
\State run$_k \gets$ hysteresis on $\max_{z\in k}(q_h, q_c)$
\State SAT$_k \gets$ \ParSatCoolHi\,K if cooling (\ParSatMixFloor\,K if also heating), 305\,K otherwise; recirc$_k \gets$ economizer
\State $F_z \gets q_c/(T_z - T_{\text{sa},k})$ or $q_h/(308.15-\delta-T_z)$; $P_k \gets P_{\text{design}}\max_z f_z^2$; damper$_z \gets f_z/\sqrt{P_k/P_{\text{design}}}$
\State $W_z \gets F_z (T_{\text{disch},z} - T_{\text{sa}})/(T_w - T_{\text{sa}})$; $\Delta p \gets (\max_z W_z / (0.9 k_v))^2$; valve$_z \gets W_z/(k_v\sqrt{\Delta p})$
\State $T_w^{\text{sp}} \gets 350$\,K if heating season else 310\,K; pump $\gets$ any valve open
\State scatter all setpoints into the normalized action vectors; clip
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Rounds 9 and 10: v11 and v12}
\label{app:note-v11}
\paragraph{Round 9: v11.}
A round-9 loss decomposition of v10 on all \RNineNDecomp{} HC2 and public scenarios, together with a perfect-foresight
linear programme on the kit's own (linear) building model, showed that v10 ran a full morning recovery every Saturday.
The day-of-week observation is an integer day sent as float32 sine and cosine; v10 decoded it with \texttt{atan2} and
compared the result with 5, and Saturday decodes to \RNineSatDecode, so Saturday counted as a workday (and the day after
Sunday, \RNineSunDecode, did not, so Monday's recovery could not start before midnight). The Saturday-morning
preconditioning carried \RNineSatShareHCTwo{} of v10's HC2 loss (\RNineSatShareHCTwoMin--\RNineSatShareHCTwoMax{} in
every scenario) and \RNineSatSharePub{} of its public-30 loss. \textbf{v11} rounds the decoded day; nothing else changes
and no parameter was tuned. It was registered before it ran on any screening or confirmation set and tested under the
rule of rounds 6--8 (public-30 not worse and HC2 better, paired bootstrap, \RNineNBoot{} resamples). HC2 was not
untouched: it had confirmed v10 in round 8, and v10's runs on it were part of the decomposition that found the bug.
HC2 (\RNineConfHarshCTwoN) \RNineConfHarshCTwoRef{} $\to$ \RNineConfHarshCTwoCand, \RNineConfHarshCTwoRel{} (95\% CI
\RNineConfHarshCTwoRelLo{} to \RNineConfHarshCTwoRelHi), \RNineConfHarshCTwoWins{} wins; public 30 \RNineConfPubRef{}
$\to$ \RNineConfPubCand, \RNineConfPubRel{} (\RNineConfPubRelLo{} to \RNineConfPubRelHi), \RNineConfPubWins; HS
(\RNineConfHarshSN) \RNineConfHarshSRel, \RNineConfHarshSWins; M (\RNineConfMinnN) \RNineConfMinnRel,
\RNineConfMinnWins; FD (\RNineConfFreshDefN{} scenarios of \RNineFDNBuild{} larger generated buildings, a stress test
outside the rule) \RNineConfFreshDefRel{} (\RNineConfFreshDefRelLo{} to \RNineConfFreshDefRelHi),
\RNineConfFreshDefWins, with \RNineFDTopTwoLosses{} of its \RNineConfFreshDefLosses{} losses on buildings
\RNineFDTopTwoBuild{} (the largest rooms and the largest floor area of their seed range; at most
\RNineConfFreshDefWorstRel).

\paragraph{Round 10: v12.}
\textbf{v12} adds three changes to v11: a deeper weekend setback (\RTenWkExtLo\,K instead of \RTenWkExtLoVEleven\,K);
a second-order online zone model that may only shorten the preconditioning lead (its recovery time
$\times$\RTenMprSafety); and a heating season of \RTenSeasonDays{} days after the last heating demand instead of
\RTenHeatSeasonDays{} (hot-water loop kept hot, night-flush guard, set-back reheat), applied only while the outside air
was below \RTenSeasonOatC\,$^\circ$C at some step of the last 24 hours. A hot-water setpoint ramp that lowered billed
gas was left out, because its saving comes from the billing gap that starter-kit pull request~\#2 closes
(Appendix~\ref{app:diag}). The components and the \RTenSeasonOatC\,$^\circ$C gate were chosen on development
scenarios, so the numbers from those runs are selected; the candidate was then registered before any run on two fresh
sets of \RTenNNewBuild{} new buildings from the shipped family. Against v11 (same rule form as above, with at least
\RTenRuleWins{} wins, \RTenRuleWinsPct, on the primary set and no guard both significantly worse and worse by more than
\RTenRuleMaterial): HC3 (\RTenConfHarshCThreeN{} cold scenarios, primary) \RTenConfHarshCThreeRef{} $\to$
\RTenConfHarshCThreeCand, \RTenConfHarshCThreeRel{} (95\% CI \RTenConfHarshCThreeRelLo{} to
\RTenConfHarshCThreeRelHi), \RTenConfHarshCThreeWins{} wins; public 30 \RTenConfPubRef{} $\to$ \RTenConfPubCand,
\RTenConfPubRel{} (\RTenConfPubRelLo{} to \RTenConfPubRelHi), \RTenConfPubWins; HH (\RTenConfHotHN{} hot scenarios,
guard) \RTenConfHotHRel{} (\RTenConfHotHRelLo{} to \RTenConfHotHRelHi), \RTenConfHotHWins{} wins and
\RTenConfHotHLosses{} losses (\RTenConfHotHTies{} unchanged), worst \RTenConfHotHWorstRel{} on a
\RTenConfHotHWorstStation{} window starting in \RTenConfHotHWorstMonth; HS \RTenConfHarshSRel, \RTenConfHarshSWins; M
\RTenConfMinnRel, \RTenConfMinnWins; FD \RTenConfFreshDefRel{} (\RTenConfFreshDefRelLo{} to \RTenConfFreshDefRelHi),
\RTenConfFreshDefWins. Comfort regret rises by \RTenComfRiseHCThree{} on HC3 and \RTenComfRisePub{} on the public 30
(from \RTenComfShareHCThreeRef{} to \RTenComfShareHCThreeCand{} and from \RTenComfSharePubRef{} to
\RTenComfSharePubCand{} of the loss); lower energy cost and carbon more than pay for it. HC3's cold windows do not
exercise the gate, which is therefore supported only by the development runs and the guards. v12 is the shipped policy
(\texttt{submission/transfer\_policy.py}, which reproduces \RTenXcheckN{} of its confirmation runs bit for bit); v11
and v10 are kept beside it. Neither v11 nor v12 has been submitted to Kaggle.
